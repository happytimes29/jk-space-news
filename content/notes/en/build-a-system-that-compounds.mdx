The internet has made it possible for anyone to find what they want online. With the rise of AI, I feel like I can find things twice as fast.

While browsing online, I noticed how much of the content felt alike. I didn't pay much attention at first. But when I thought about it today, I found myself wondering: Why is there so much surface-level content, while ideas that make you think are so hard to find?

Why?

Thinking it over, I realized that the easiest things to find tend to be the ones people like to watch and that draw a lot of traffic. Creators often use a three-second hook to get you to click. By the time you've finished watching, it feels like you've sat through a whole explanation and come away with nothing.

The genuinely useful material is often behind a paywall. This kind of free content mostly gets you to watch an ad and gives you little guidance you can actually use.

Even when you pay for a course, it's easy to get stuck once you try to apply it. Two people can hear or watch the same lesson and come away with different interpretations.

It's also hard to fit what a course teaches into the way you already work or learn.

The information can be useful. It just takes time to understand it, put it into practice, and make it part of what you know. When something goes wrong in practice, you have to adjust what you do. Buying a lot of courses doesn't help much if you never have time to absorb what they teach or make it your own.

So what can we actually do? Here are a few ideas I'd like to try.

1. Think through what the course teaches and turn it into a process you can follow.
2. Build a feedback loop. Define a measurable standard for each step so you can review it later.
3. When you review, put the numbers in a dashboard that's easy to read. Record the changes you make to the steps so you can check whether they actually work.
4. Make the system build on what you've already learned, then let it guide what you do next.

Using a system to guide your work can make it easier to follow through. It also gives you a way to keep collecting data, use it to refine the system, and improve little by little.
